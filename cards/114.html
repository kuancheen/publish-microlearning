Two Paths to the Same Celestial City

💡 Core Takeaway

The classic allegory of Christian faith is now available in two distinct graphic novel styles, each offering a unique visual entry point into Bunyan’s timeless story.

In a single month, publishers released two major graphic novel adaptations of John Bunyan’s Pilgrim’s Progress, a testament to the enduring power of this 17th-century allegory. This surge in new editions signals that the core narrative of spiritual struggle and perseverance remains deeply relevant for modern readers, who are increasingly seeking faith-based content in accessible, visual formats.

The two books approach the same journey with different visual languages. Christian’s Quest, from Lithos Kids, uses clean, polished lines and soft, muted colors to create a welcoming atmosphere for younger readers, lightly updating archaic terms to make the story feel immediate. In contrast, Crossway’s Pilgrim’s Progress: The Graphic Novel employs gritty, heavy brushwork and dark shadows to depict a gaunt, weathered pilgrim, preserving the original text’s weight and intensity for a teen and adult audience. Both versions remain faithful to Bunyan’s plot, but they invite different emotional responses: one offers clarity and comfort, while the other emphasizes the raw difficulty of the walk.

This choice matters because the way a story is presented shapes how we internalize its lessons. Whether you are guiding a child’s first encounter with spiritual allegory or seeking a fresh perspective on your own faith journey, these adaptations provide a tangible way to engage with the text. The visual medium allows the abstract concepts of the Slough of Despond or the Delectable Mountains to become concrete, memorable images that can anchor your spiritual reflection.

Try it today: Pick one of the two books and spend five minutes reading the opening chapter. Notice how the art style changes your perception of Christian’s burden. Does the clean style make the journey feel manageable, or does the gritty style make the struggle feel more real? Let that observation guide your prayer for the day.
🤔 Which visual style helps you see the weight of your own spiritual burdens more clearly?

Source: Challies, "Spoiled for Choice: Two New Graphic Novels of Pilgrim’s Progress"
