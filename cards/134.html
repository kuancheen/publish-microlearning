The Coffee Pot Principle: Small Acts, Big Impact

💡 Core Takeaway

Relationships thrive not on grand gestures, but on consistently noticing and appreciating the small, daily efforts your partner makes.

Drs. John and Julie Gottman, renowned relationship researchers, identify "turning toward" your partner as the single most important habit for a healthy marriage. In their work, they highlight how a simple act—like making coffee for a partner who sleeps in—can become a source of conflict if it goes unnoticed, but a source of deep connection when acknowledged. The core principle is that love is built through ordinary exchanges, not just during crises. When you respond to a "bid for connection," whether it is a question, a comment, or a request for attention, you signal that your partner matters to you in the mundane moments of daily life.

This matters now because modern life often pulls us into separate orbits, where we coordinate schedules but stop asking about each other’s inner worlds. The Gottmans call this knowledge of your partner’s current feelings and stresses a "Love Map." Keeping this map current requires asking open-ended questions and deliberately looking for what your partner is doing right, rather than focusing on what they are missing. This positive perspective acts as an antidote to the negativity bias that often creeps into long-term relationships. In practice, this means thanking your spouse for handling the morning routine or admiring how they listen to your children, rather than waiting for a major milestone to express appreciation.

Try it today: Next time your partner makes a small effort, such as bringing you a drink or handling a chore, pause and say specifically what you appreciate. Do not just say "thanks," but name the action and how it helped you. This takes less than a minute but reinforces the bond.
🤔 What is one small thing your partner did this week that you have not yet acknowledged?
