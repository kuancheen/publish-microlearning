The Order of Love: Why Boundaries Are an Act of Grace

💡 Core Takeaway

True love is not the absence of boundaries, but the disciplined ordering of our affections to reflect God’s design.

Alex Kocman argues that Augustine’s concept of ordo amoris, or the order of love, is essential for Christian life. This principle suggests we must direct our affections in a specific sequence: first to God, then to our families, and finally outward to the wider world. Kocman warns that studying love without practical application is lifeless, urging believers to move from theory to daily exhortation.

In a culture that often conflates love with the removal of all friction, this ordered approach offers a counter-cultural truth. It matters now because many churches and individuals struggle with the pressure to "just love everyone" without distinction. Kocman’s perspective reminds us that rightly ordered love requires us to build walls, make distinctions, and establish boundaries. These are not signs of coldness, but of clarity. When we love God first, our capacity to love our spouses and children deeply is protected, and our love for neighbors becomes sustainable rather than performative.

This plays out in real life by helping us resist the urge to overextend ourselves to the point of burnout or resentment. It means saying no to a good cause so we can say yes to a critical family obligation. It means recognizing that our thoughts and actions are shaped by what we prioritize. By anchoring our love in God, we avoid the trap of seeking validation from others, allowing our relationships to be grounded in truth rather than mere sentiment.

Try it today: Spend five minutes in prayer listing the three people you love most. Ask God to reveal if your time and energy are aligned with that order, and identify one boundary you need to set to protect that priority.
🤔 Where in your life is your love out of order, and what would it look like to realign it with God’s design?

Source: Alex Kocman, 4 Applications for the Order of Love—From the Home to the Ends of the Earth
