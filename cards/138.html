Planting the Seed of Mission in Young Hearts

💡 Core Takeaway

Sharing the stories of historical missionaries helps children understand that the Great Commission is a living reality, not just a concept.

The Goers series, developed in partnership with Missionary, introduces children to the true stories of men and women sent by churches to spread the good news of Jesus Christ. These illustrated biographies feature figures like William Tyndale, who fled across Europe with a bounty on his head to translate the Bible, and Amy Carmichael, who rescued and raised girls in India. By presenting these accounts through the eyes of young characters, the books bridge the gap between historical events and a child’s daily life, showing that the love for God’s word connects generations.

Why does this matter now? In a world where faith can feel abstract or distant, grounding children in the concrete sacrifices of past believers makes the call to mission tangible. It shifts the narrative from passive belief to active participation. When a child learns that someone like David Livingstone stood for justice in Africa, or that Tyndale risked everything for a translation, they begin to see themselves as part of a larger, ongoing story. This fosters a sense of belonging and purpose that extends beyond the local church.

In practice, this means moving beyond simple memorization of verses. It involves storytelling that invites reflection. Parents can use these narratives to discuss how faith looks in action, whether in a farm field or a foreign land. It encourages children to ask how they can serve, not just what they believe. This approach builds a foundation for a lifelong commitment to the Great Commission, rooted in the example of those who went before.

Try it today: Pick one missionary story from the Goers series or a similar source. Share it with your child over dinner or bedtime, and ask one simple question: "What part of their story made you feel brave?"
