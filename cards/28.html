Which Gospel Writer Most Inspired Paul’s Theology?

🗂️ SPIRITUAL DEPTH & CHRISTIAN GROWTH | ⏱️ 3-Min Microlearning 📌 STACK: The Johannine Lens: Seeing Paul Through John

📖 CARD 1: CONTEXT & ORIGIN > "Did you know that the Apostle Paul, often viewed as the primary architect of Christian theology, may have actually been shaped by the Gospel of John rather than the other way around?" • Source & Author: Andrew Wilson, Teaching Pastor at King’s Church London and author of Remaking the World. This insight is drawn from a review of Michael J. Gorman’s scholarly work, Paul and John in Harmony. • Genesis: Written to challenge the standard academic assumption that Paul’s letters predate and influenced the Gospels. Gorman argues for a "Johannine Paul," suggesting that the deep mystical and relational theology of John’s Gospel provided the foundation for Paul’s later epistles, particularly in how he describes the believer’s union with Christ.

💡 CARD 2: THE CORE EPIPHANY > "Paul is not just a legalist arguing for justification; he is a mystic describing a mutual indwelling with Christ." • Why it matters: This shifts your view of Paul from a dry, argumentative teacher to a man deeply immersed in the intimate, relational theology of John. It reveals that the "legal" arguments in Romans and Galatians are grounded in the "relational" reality of "You are in me and I am in you."

🛠️ CARD 3: THE TACTICAL PRINCIPLE • Key Concept: Theology of Mutual Indwelling (John 15 / Philippians 2) • Execution: Reframe Your Identity: Stop viewing your faith as a list of rules you must follow to earn favor. View it as a state of being "in" Christ. Your actions flow out of that union, not toward* it. * Study the Parallel: When reading a difficult Pauline epistle (like Philippians), cross-reference it with the corresponding scene in John’s Gospel (like the foot-washing in John 13). Look for the same language of "emptying oneself" and "love to the end." * Prayer Shift: Move from petitionary prayer (asking for things) to contemplative prayer (dwelling in the presence of the One who dwells in you).

🎯 CARD 4: THE MICRO-CHALLENGE • Today's Action: Open your Bible to Philippians 2:5-8 and John 13:1-17. Read them side-by-side. Identify one specific phrase in Paul that mirrors the action or language of Jesus in John. Write that phrase on a sticky note and place it on your mirror or computer screen as a reminder that your service to others stems from Christ’s self-emptying love, not your own effort.

💬 CARD 5: REFLECT & INTERNALIZE • Daily Question: "Am I trying to earn my place in the Kingdom through performance, or am I resting in the indwelling presence of Christ who has already secured it?"
