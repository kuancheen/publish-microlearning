The Trump Administration Has Resettled Zero Religious Refugees This Year

🗂️ SPIRITUAL DEPTH & CHRISTIAN GROWTH | ⏱️ 3-Min Microlearning 📌 STACK: The Cost of Silence: When Comfort Replaces Compassion

📖 CARD 1: CONTEXT & ORIGIN > "Did you know that for the first time in decades, the United States is on track to admit zero religious refugees this fiscal year, despite over 4,800 Christians being killed for their faith in the last 12 months?" • Source & Author: Report by World Relief and Open Doors US, featuring testimony from Pastor Ara Torosian (fled Iran in 2010) and data from Open Doors’ 2026 World Watch List. • Genesis: Written in response to the stark reality that while global persecution of Christians is at an all-time high, the US resettlement program has effectively closed its doors to those fleeing religious oppression, prioritizing other demographic groups instead.

💡 CARD 2: THE CORE EPIPHANY > "A faith that protects its own comfort while ignoring the suffering of the global Body of Christ is not biblical stewardship; it is spiritual isolationism." • Why it matters: We often assume that political alignment or national identity guarantees the protection of the persecuted church. The data reveals a dangerous gap between our theological beliefs (70-82% of evangelicals believe we have a moral duty to welcome refugees) and our political reality, sending a message that the suffering of believers abroad is not a priority.

🛠️ CARD 3: THE TACTICAL PRINCIPLE • Key Concept: Active Solidarity Over Passive Assumption (Based on Matthew 25:35-40 and Romans 12:15). • Execution: * Verify the Narrative: Do not assume that "Christian-friendly" political rhetoric equals tangible action for the persecuted church. Check the data (Open Doors, World Relief) to see where the body of Christ is actually bleeding. * Shift from Consumption to Advocacy: Move beyond liking social media posts about persecution. Identify one specific organization (like World Relief or Open Doors) and make a tangible financial or advocacy commitment to them this week. * Pray with Specificity: Stop praying generic prayers for "persecuted Christians." Pray for the specific families mentioned in reports (e.g., the Karen minority in Myanmar, the Iranian converts) and for the conscience of those in power.

🎯 CARD 4: THE MICRO-CHALLENGE • Today's Action: Spend 3 minutes reading the "Stories" section of the World Relief/Open Doors report (or a similar recent report). Pick one specific person or family mentioned. Write their name and their specific struggle on a sticky note and place it on your bathroom mirror or dashboard. For the rest of the day, whenever you see it, offer a 10-second prayer for their specific situation.

💬 CARD 5: REFLECT & INTERNALIZE • Daily Question: "Am I so comfortable in my own freedom that I have become indifferent to the cost of that freedom for my brothers and sisters halfway across the world?"
