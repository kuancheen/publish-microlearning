Reclaiming the Sacred Rhythm of Rest

💡 Core Takeaway

True rest is not about owning your time, but about surrendering it to God.

Zak Mellgren challenges the common greeting "How was your weekend?" by arguing that the phrase reinforces a false sense of ownership over our time. While asking about someone's week is a kind gesture that opens doors for deeper connection, the underlying assumption that the weekend belongs to us can subtly distort our spiritual posture. In a culture that treats rest as a commodity to be earned or a vacation to be optimized, this linguistic habit keeps us tethered to a mindset of control rather than trust.

The core principle here is that rest is not merely the absence of work, but a spiritual discipline of surrender. When we view the weekend as "ours," we are often just shifting from one set of obligations to another, or using the time to compensate for stress through consumption. However, when we view it as God's gift, the dynamic changes. We stop trying to maximize productivity or relaxation and start looking for moments of stillness that point us back to the Creator. This shift transforms the weekend from a break in the schedule into a rehearsal for the eternal rest that awaits believers.

In practice, this means intentionally creating space for silence, prayer, or simple presence with family without the pressure to "do" anything. It is about recognizing that your energy and attention are not your own to hoard, but are entrusted to you for stewardship. By releasing the grip of ownership, you allow the weekend to become a place of spiritual renewal rather than just a pause in the grind.

Try it today: Before you start your weekend, spend five minutes in silence. Instead of making a to-do list, write down one thing you are willing to let go of for God's sake, and pray for the grace to release it.
🤔 What is one area of your life where you are still trying to be the owner rather than the steward?

Source: Zak Mellgren, 'There’s No Such Thing as “Your” Weekend,' via Weekend A La Carte (Challies.com)
