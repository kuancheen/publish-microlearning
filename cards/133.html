The Power of the Six-Page Memo

💡 Core Takeaway

Replacing slide decks with written memos forces clarity and exposes weak ideas before they reach the board.

Bing Gordon, former Chief Creative Officer of Electronic Arts and a long-time Amazon board member, credits his seat on the board to offering Jeff Bezos five strategic ideas, the first of which was Amazon Prime. Gordon highlights a specific operational shift Bezos implemented to improve decision-making: replacing slide decks with six-page memos. This change was not merely aesthetic; it was a tool to force writers to articulate their logic clearly and to allow readers to engage with the argument at their own pace, rather than passively watching a presentation.

The core principle here is that writing is thinking. When you are forced to write a narrative, you cannot hide behind bullet points or visual distractions. Gordon notes that Bezos opened meetings by sharing what was on his mind, creating a culture of transparency and directness. This approach helps avoid the "institutional no," where a company fails to act because no single person takes ownership of the idea. By demanding clear, written arguments, leaders can identify where the thinking is fuzzy and where the data is weak before resources are committed. In practical terms, this means that whether you are pitching a new product, proposing a policy change, or even planning a family vacation, the quality of your written plan often predicts the quality of the outcome.

Try it today: Take your next major decision or proposal and write it out in a two-page narrative. Do not use bullet points. Force yourself to connect the dots with full sentences. If you find yourself struggling to explain a step, that is where your thinking is incomplete.
🤔 What is one idea you have been avoiding writing down because you are afraid it will look weak on paper?
