Ryan Serhant: How to Get People to Say Yes

🗂️ PERSONAL MASTERY & PROFESSIONAL EDGE | ⏱️ 3-Min Microlearning 📌 STACK: Earn the Right to Ask

📖 CARD 1: CONTEXT & ORIGIN > "Did you know that the most successful negotiators in New York real estate don't start by pitching their product, but by diagnosing the client's pain before they even mention a price tag?" • Source & Author: Ryan Serhant, founder of SERHANT. and star of Million Dollar Listing New York. He is one of the highest-grossing real estate brokers in the world, known for high-stakes negotiations in Manhattan. • Genesis: This insight comes from his appearance on the Knowledge Project podcast, where he breaks down the psychology of persuasion. The context is the rejection of "fake it till you make it" and the realization that ego kills deals. He argues that you cannot sell what you haven't earned the right to offer.

💡 CARD 2: THE CORE EPIPHANY > "You do not have the right to ask for a 'yes' until you have proven you are the solution to their specific problem." • Why it matters: Most people approach interactions with a transactional mindset (I want X, give me Y). Serhant flips this: if you haven't built a genuine connection and demonstrated value first, your ask is noise. People resist being sold to; they align with those who help them.

🛠️ CARD 3: THE TACTICAL PRINCIPLE • Key Concept: The "Help First" Protocol (or Earning the Ask). • Execution: * Diagnose Before Prescribing: In any negotiation or request, spend the first 30% of the conversation listening. Ask questions that uncover their underlying fear or desire, not just their surface-level need. * Strip the Ego: Before you speak, ask yourself: "Am I saying this to look smart, or to help them?" If it’s the former, shut up. Ego signals that you care more about the win than the relationship. * The 1,000-Minute Audit: Treat your day as a bank of 1,000 minutes. Before you spend 10 minutes on a sales pitch, ensure you’ve spent 20 minutes building trust or solving a small problem for that person.

🎯 CARD 4: THE MICRO-CHALLENGE • Today's Action: Identify one person you need to say "no" to or ask for a favor from. Before you send the email or make the call, write down one specific, low-cost way you can help them or add value to their life today without asking for anything in return. Do that first.

💬 CARD 5: REFLECT & INTERNALIZE • Daily Question: "In my last three interactions, did I listen to understand, or did I listen to reply? Did I earn the right to ask, or did I just assume I had it?"

Source: Ryan Serhant: How to Get People to Say Yes
