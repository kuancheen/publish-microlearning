The Body Speaks What Words Hide

💡 Core Takeaway

True connection requires witnessing the full human experience, not just analyzing the data.

A Gottman therapist recently described a client who appeared remarkably calm during video sessions, yet when they finally met in person, the therapist noticed the woman vigorously petting a dog and tapping her foot. This physical tension revealed an emotional reality that the screen had completely hidden. The therapist didn't diagnose her; they simply shared the observation, inviting her to explore what her body was trying to say. This distinction highlights a critical limit of technology: it can process information, but it cannot witness the subtle, non-verbal cues that define how two people actually relate to one another.

In marriage and parenting, we often mistake intellectual understanding for emotional connection. We can read about conflict resolution or even ask an AI for scripts on how to apologize, but knowing the theory doesn't stop us from criticizing when we are upset. Real change happens in the messy, in-the-moment practice of slowing down. It requires noticing when a partner’s breathing changes or when a child’s posture shifts, and having the presence to ask, "What is happening for you right now?" rather than assuming we already know the answer. The goal isn't to be right; it's to be curious.

Try it today: In your next conversation with your partner or child, stop focusing on the words being spoken. Instead, watch their hands, their posture, and their breathing for one full minute. Notice one small physical cue that doesn't match their verbal tone, and gently name what you see without judging it.
🤔 Where in your relationships are you listening for the answer, but missing the question?

Source: The Gottman Institute, "What Technology Can Not Replace in Therapy"
