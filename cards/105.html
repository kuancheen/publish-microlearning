Professing Christ Is Not the Same as Possessing Him

💡 Core Takeaway

True discipleship begins with supernatural regeneration, not just a decision to believe.

Karl Clauson argues that the Western church faces a deeper crisis than weak discipleship: it has redefined conversion itself. After forty years in ministry, he observes that many believers have traded supernatural regeneration for human decision, resulting in a generation that is deeply Christianized but not necessarily changed. They know the songs and the rituals, yet many have never been brought from death to life by the power of God.

When salvation is reduced to a prayer to repeat or a card to sign, discipleship collapses under the weight of human effort. Following Jesus becomes a matter of behavior management and moral striving rather than a life powered by grace. This creates a gap where people assume their current state is all God has for them, settling for a cultural Christianity that lacks the transformative power of the true gospel. The result is a faith that informs but does not change, leaving believers in a fog of confusion rather than in the freedom of a flourishing soul.

Try it today: Spend five minutes in honest prayer. Ask God to reveal any areas where you are relying on your own effort to follow Him rather than resting in His power. Write down one specific habit or belief that needs to be surrendered to true regeneration.
🤔 Where in your life are you trying to disciple yourself instead of allowing Christ to possess you?

Source: Karl Clauson, The Unstoppable Gospel, Moody Publishers
