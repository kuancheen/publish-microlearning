The Art of Dying Well

💡 Core Takeaway

Christian hope transforms the fear of death into a confident expectation of resurrection.

Robert Kolb’s new book, We Too Shall Arise, recovers the medieval and Reformation tradition of the ars moriendi, or the art of dying. For centuries, Christians developed specific guides to help believers face their final moments not with terror of judgment, but with confidence in Christ’s resurrection. This tradition, which Martin Luther himself deepened through hymns and pastoral guides, offers a structured way to prepare for life’s inevitable end.

In a culture that often ignores, fears, or postpones the reality of death, this perspective is urgently needed. Kolb argues that by focusing on the risen Jesus, who shines brighter than the thickest darkness, we can walk through the valley of the shadow of death with comfort rather than despair. The core principle is that our hope is not abstract; it is anchored in the historical reality of the resurrection, which changes how we live and how we die.

Try it today: Spend five minutes reading Psalm 23. Instead of skimming, sit with the phrase "I will fear no evil." Ask yourself what specific fears are currently weighing on you, and consciously replace each one with the promise that the Shepherd is with you in the valley.
🤔 What is one fear you can hand to Christ today, trusting that His resurrection is brighter than your darkness?

Source: Robert Kolb, Baker Books, We Too Shall Arise
