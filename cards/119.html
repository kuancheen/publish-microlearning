The Power of 'What's Coming Up?'

💡 Core Takeaway

Asking 'What's coming up for you?' invites connection without triggering defensiveness.

Jefferson Fisher, a Texas father and attorney, argues that most people struggle with communication not because of external forces, but because it is simply hard work. In his book The Next Conversation, he emphasizes that effective dialogue requires practice and discipline, noting that we rarely get it entirely right. His core principle is that how we speak to our children will shape how they speak to theirs, making our daily words a legacy in the making.

Fisher suggests a small but powerful shift in phrasing to reduce conflict. Instead of asking "What's wrong?" or "What's up?"—which often puts people on the defensive—he recommends asking, "What's coming up for you?" This subtle change frames the conversation around shared experience rather than fault. He also highlights the importance of "repair" after a rupture, which involves owning your words and validating your partner's feelings, even when you disagree. For him, fatherhood is a daily devotion to inconveniencing yourself for your family's comfort, like carrying the bags in the airport so others don't have to.

Try it today: The next time your partner or child seems distracted or tense, skip the standard "How are you?" and ask, "What's coming up for you today?" Listen to the answer without immediately offering a fix or judgment. Just let them share what is on their mind.

Source: Jefferson Fisher, Fatherly
