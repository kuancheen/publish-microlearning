The Table Is Your Teaching Moment

💡 Core Takeaway

Ordinary family game nights can become powerful opportunities to discuss Scripture when we intentionally create space for curiosity.

Marco Bartholomae, founder of Doxa Games, recalls a moment while playing cards with his children where he realized that shared playtime could be a vehicle for spiritual conversation. He notes that Deuteronomy 6 commands believers to speak of God’s commandments to their children during ordinary activities, such as sitting at home. While games do not replace formal family devotions, they provide a low-pressure context where children might naturally ask questions about biblical figures or events, opening the door for deeper discussion.

This approach aligns with the concept of Doxa, the Greek word for glory, which Bartholomae uses to name his company. Drawing from 1 Corinthians 10:31, the principle is that even mundane activities like eating, drinking, or playing can be done to God’s glory. By integrating faith into leisure, parents model that their relationship with God is not confined to church services but permeates their daily lives. This makes the faith tangible and relatable for children who see their parents engaging with Scripture in a relaxed, joyful setting.

Try it today: Next time your family gathers for a board game or card game, pause for one minute before starting. Ask your children to name one thing they are grateful for or one question they have about God. Keep it brief and light, then proceed with the game. This small pause signals that the time together is intentional and sacred.
🤔 What ordinary activity in your home could become a consistent opportunity to talk about your faith?

Source: Marco Bartholomae, "What If Family Game Night Got Your Kids Talking About Scripture?", Challies.com (Sponsored by Doxa Games)
