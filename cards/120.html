Discipleship Cannot Be Delegated

💡 Core Takeaway

While you can outsource childcare and schooling, the responsibility to raise children who follow Jesus remains uniquely yours.

Kyle Borg argues that the Bible places the primary responsibility for discipling children squarely on their parents, a duty that supersedes all other provisions a household can offer. In a culture where many aspects of family life are outsourced, this specific task is the one thing you cannot hand off to a school, a church program, or a relative. It is the ultimate priority of the Christian home.

This distinction matters because it clarifies what is optional and what is essential. You may delegate childcare, choose schooling based on your conscience, and allow others to offer counsel. However, the act of teaching your children to love the Lord more than they love their parents is a personal covenant. It requires your direct presence, your voice, and your consistent example. When we treat discipleship as a service to be purchased rather than a relationship to be built, we miss the core of our parental calling.

In practice, this means carving out time for intentional spiritual conversation, not just logistical management. It is about modeling faith in the mundane moments and ensuring your children see Jesus as the center of your life, not just a topic of discussion. It is a significant responsibility, but it is also the most profound gift you can give them.

Try it today: Set a timer for five minutes. Sit with your child or a young person in your life and ask one specific question about their faith or a Bible verse they are studying. Listen without correcting, just to understand their current understanding.
🤔 What is one area of your child’s spiritual life where you have been relying on others instead of stepping in yourself?

Source: Kyle Borg, 'Teach Your Children to Hate You,' via Weekend A La Carte (November 14, 2026)
