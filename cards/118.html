The Habits Scorecard: See What You're Actually Doing

💡 Core Takeaway

You cannot change what you do not consciously see.

Japanese train operators use a technique called Pointing-and-Calling, where they point at signals and call out their status to reduce errors by up to 85 percent. This ritual works because it forces a nonconscious action into the spotlight of awareness. We often assume our daily routines are fine because they are familiar, but familiarity breeds blindness. We repeat behaviors on autopilot, missing the small cues that tell us when a habit is serving us or sabotaging us. The Habits Scorecard is a simple tool to break that autopilot. It asks you to list your daily actions and mark each one as positive, neutral, or based on whether it aligns with the person you want to become. The goal isn’t to fix everything at once. It is to observe your life with the same detached clarity as a train operator. When you name your habits, you stop being a passenger and start being the driver. This shift in awareness is the first and most crucial step in any meaningful change.

Try it today: Spend five minutes writing down ten things you did this morning. Mark each with a plus, minus, or equal sign based on whether it moved you forward or held you back. Do not try to change anything yet. Just watch.
🤔 Which one of your automatic habits is quietly working against the person you are trying to become?

Source: James C.clear, Atomic Habits
