Real Fear vs. Anxious Noise

💡 Core Takeaway

You can distinguish between the tangible fear of the present moment and the anxious noise of the future by observing what feels physically heavy and immediate.

Actor Max Greenfield, who plays a father navigating his daughter’s high school years and his wife’s cancer diagnosis in the film Don’t Say Good Luck, shares a practical method for managing parental anxiety. He explains that as his children have grown, the line between responsibility and letting go has become thinner, often leading him to operate from a place of fear. He realized that trying to control every outcome to feel safe was not a healthy zone for parenting.

Greenfield identifies a clear distinction between two types of fear. He describes the anxiety about the future, work, or life in general as "not real" because it is abstract and unmanageable. In contrast, he points to the moment his daughter is behind the wheel as "real fear." This fear is tangible, heavy, and appropriate for the specific situation. By recognizing that the shark-in-the-water fear is the only one that demands his full attention, he can dismiss the background noise of worry. This allows him to be present for the actual moment rather than paralyzed by hypotheticals.

This principle applies to any high-stakes parenting moment, from a first date to a college application. Instead of spiraling into what could go wrong, you can ask yourself if the fear is tangible and immediate. If it is, you address it directly. If it is abstract, you let it go. This shift reduces the mental load and helps you model calm for your children.

Try it today: Next time you feel a surge of worry about your child’s future, pause and ask: "Is this fear tangible and happening right now, or is it a story I am telling myself?" If it is the latter, take one deep breath and release it.
🤔 Which worry is currently taking up the most space in your mind, and is it a real fear or just noise?
