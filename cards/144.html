The Power God Chooses to Use

💡 Core Takeaway

God’s absolute power is limitless, but His ordained power is the specific, reliable force He has chosen to exercise in your life.

Theologians have long distinguished between what God can do and what God does do. This distinction separates His absolute power—His ability to do whatever He pleases without limit—from His ordained power, which is the power He has actually chosen to exercise. As the 17th-century divine George Swinnock noted, “God does not do many things that He can.” He could have created a second earth, altered the laws of gravity, or wiped out humanity at the time of the Flood. He could have, but He did not. Instead, He exercises His power in a way that ensures He accomplishes all the things He desires and determines, with no force or creature able to hinder Him.

This matters now because it shifts your focus from what God could have done to what He is doing. When you face a situation that feels out of control, the temptation is to wonder why God isn’t intervening in a way you expect. But His ordained power is not random; it is intentional. He is not limited by your circumstances, but He is bound by His own character and promises. He does all things that He will, meaning His actions are always aligned with His perfect will, not just His raw capability.

In real life, this means trusting the process even when the outcome isn't what you imagined. Whether you are navigating a difficult marriage, a career transition, or a season of prayer, remember that God’s power is not a free-for-all. It is a directed force. He is working toward a specific end that He has determined. Your role is not to force His hand but to rest in the certainty that He is accomplishing His purposes, unhindered by any obstacle.

Try it today: Spend five minutes in quiet prayer listing three things you are currently anxious about. For each one, write down a specific promise from Scripture where God states what He will do, rather than what He could do. Sit in silence for one minute after each, trusting His ordained power over the situation.
🤔 Where in your life are you waiting for God to act in a way that aligns with His will rather than your expectations?
