The World Is Always Burning

💡 Core Takeaway

Constant anxiety about global chaos often stems from mistaking current visibility for historical novelty.

Andrea Sanborn observes that while it feels like the world is falling apart right now, the truth is that the world has always been burning. She notes that technology allows us to witness the flames in every corner, creating a low-level anxiety in our spirits as we wonder how close the fire will creep to our homes. This constant stream of bad news can make us feel that we are falling apart from each other, from common sense, and even from God.

The core principle here is that the perception of unprecedented danger is often a product of our connectivity, not a change in the world's fundamental nature. For the believer, this shifts the focus from reacting to every headline to trusting in God's sovereignty over a history that has always included droughts, floods, wars, and riots. It matters now because it frees us from the paralysis of trying to fix every global crisis and allows us to anchor our peace in something more stable than the news cycle.

In real life, this means curating your information intake. You don't have to be ignorant of the world, but you don't have to be consumed by it. Whether you are parenting, working, or praying, recognizing that this anxiety is a modern amplification of an ancient reality helps you respond with wisdom rather than panic.

Try it today: Set a timer for five minutes. During that time, turn off all news feeds and social media. Sit in silence and write down one specific thing you are grateful for that is not dependent on the world's current state. Let that gratitude be your anchor for the rest of the day.
🤔 What is one source of anxiety you can intentionally disconnect from today to make room for peace?

Source: Andrea Sanborn, 'The World Is Always Burning' (November 16, 2026), via Challies.com
